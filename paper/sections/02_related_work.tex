\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Irregular time-series representation learning, and what it assumes about observations.}
Interpolation-based methods map observations onto a regular grid before encoding \cite{shukla2019ipn};
continuous-time methods evolve a latent state between observations
\cite{rubanova2019latentode,debrouwer2019gruodebayes,kidger2020ncde}; set-based methods aggregate an unordered
observation set with attention \cite{horn2020seft,shukla2021mtand}; graph-based methods model inter-variable
structure \cite{zhang2022raindrop,zhang2024tpatchgnn}; query-based embeddings pool the observations of a variable
or temporal patch into a fixed-size token so that standard backbones \cite{liu2024itransformer,nie2023patchtst}
can be reused \cite{lim2026quite}. These designs differ in how faithfully they represent a given observation set,
and all of them are trained and evaluated under a single observation process. We do not propose a new encoder; we
ask how such an encoder behaves when that process changes, and how training can prepare it.

\paragraph{Missingness mechanisms, and why a rate is not enough.}
The statistical literature classifies missingness by its dependence on observed and unobserved values
\cite{rubin1976missing}, and in clinical data the pattern of missingness is itself predictive
\cite{lipton2016informative,che2018grud}. Learning work generally treats irregularity as a fixed property of a
dataset and probes robustness, if at all, by varying a scalar missingness \emph{rate}. Our measurements show that
this is not a sufficient description: processes that remove the same fraction of observations differ in error by
up to $3.8\times$, and which structure matters depends on the sensing modality. We therefore treat the
observation process as a structured object that can be sampled, constrained, searched over and shifted.

\paragraph{Robustness by exposure, robustness by constraint.}
Randomising nuisance factors during training underpins sim-to-real transfer \cite{tobin2017domainrand} and
time-series augmentation \cite{wen2021augmentation}; selecting difficult examples sharpens training
\cite{shrivastava2016ohem}; and training against a constrained worst case is the basis of adversarial robustness
\cite{madry2018towards}. Our views randomise or search over the measurement process rather than perturbing the
signal, which keeps every perturbation physically meaningful: each candidate is a valid way the same trajectory
could have been recorded. Invariant-risk formulations instead constrain the representation across environments
\cite{arjovsky2019irm}. We deliberately impose no cross-view representation equality: objectives of that form
need explicit anti-collapse machinery \cite{chen2020simclr,grill2020byol,bardes2022vicreg}, and the property we
require is that different observations of one trajectory remain \emph{useful} for the task, not that they map to
the same point.

\paragraph{Privileged information and distillation.}
Side information available only during training defines the LUPI setting \cite{vapnik2015lupi}; distillation
\cite{hinton2015distilling} is one way to realise it, and the two are formally connected
\cite{lopezpaz2016unifying}. Our teacher is not a stronger model in the usual sense: it has the same capacity and
objective as the student and differs only in receiving a richer observation of the same trajectory, which makes
the setting self-distillation with an asymmetry in the observation rather than in the learner. Existing methods
primarily improve the representation of the observations a model is given; we instead study the case where the
observation process itself changes, and exploit richer training-time observations as privileged information.
